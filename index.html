Safety + access

These goals do not have to be in conflict. Residents should understand the options, the data, and the potential consequences before major street changes are implemented.

Our position

We can support traffic safety without supporting every traffic-control proposal.

This website is intended to help University Park residents follow the discussion around traffic diverters and other traffic-control concepts, review public information, and participate in decisions affecting neighborhood streets.

🗣️

Meaningful public input

Residents should have clear opportunities to understand proposals and share their perspectives before major changes move forward.

📊

Data-driven decisions

Traffic interventions should be evaluated using clear data, stated goals, and measurable results.

🔄

Consider all impacts

A change that improves one intersection may affect nearby streets. Those potential impacts deserve careful consideration.

Alternatives worth evaluating

The Town's materials identify several traffic-control and calming approaches. Residents can ask the Town to compare their costs, safety benefits and unintended impacts before relying on permanent diverters.

Speed management

Use targeted speed humps, raised treatments and other proven designs where speeding is documented, rather than closing a key connection for all drivers.

Targeted signage and restrictions

Improve route guidance and test time-specific or location-specific controls where appropriate, with careful monitoring before making permanent changes.

Enforcement and safer intersections

Focus enforcement on documented stop-sign violations and speeding, and improve crossings and intersections for pedestrians and cyclists without severing neighborhood routes.

Questions worth asking

Good public policy starts with clear answers.

Will traffic simply move to another residential street?

When access is restricted at one location, residents should understand how traffic patterns may change elsewhere and how those effects will be measured.

What alternatives have been considered?

Residents should be able to compare diverters with other approaches such as traffic calming, intersection improvements, enforcement, or other measures discussed in public materials.

How will success be defined?

Before implementation, the Town should clearly identify the safety and traffic outcomes it intends to achieve and how it will evaluate results.

What opportunities will residents have to comment?

Public meetings, committee discussions, agendas, minutes, and Council decisions should be easy for residents to find and follow.
